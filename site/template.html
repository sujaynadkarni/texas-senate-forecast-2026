    How to read this

    • Turnout shows where votes come from, not whom they are for. Texas has no party registration.
    • Higher turnout in a blue county helps __D__ only if the extra voters are Democrats, and higher turnout in a red county helps __R__ only if they are Republicans. Neither is guaranteed.
    • Since 2020 many Republicans vote early instead of on Election Day, so a strong early vote in red counties may just be the same voters voting sooner.
    • Mail ballots come from voters 65 and older, voters with disabilities and a few others, so they skew older.
    • Counties report on their own schedules. A county with no numbers yet is marked "Not reported" and left out of comparisons.
    • The early vote does not move the forecast needle. The needle moves on counted votes on Nov. 3.

    How this works

    • We run 50,000 simulated elections across all 254 counties. Each starts from the polling average, adds a statewide polling error (about 4.5 points on the margin, with fat tails), regional swings and county noise.
    • County patterns blend the __HA_LABEL__ and __HB_LABEL__ results. Hispanic South and West Texas get double uncertainty after swinging about 9.5 points between those years.
    • Turnout uses each county's 2018 turnout rate applied to 2026 registration from the Texas Secretary of State.
    • On election night, counted votes from each county update the simulations, and the needle moves.
    • Backtest: run as of 2018 and 2024 using only earlier data, the model favored the eventual winner both times, and the final margin fell inside its 80% range.
