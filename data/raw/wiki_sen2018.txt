The '''2018 United States Senate election in Texas''' was held on November 6, 2018, to elect a member of the [[United States Senate]] to represent the state of [[Texas]]. [[Republican Party (United States)|Republican]] incumbent [[Ted Cruz]] won re-election to a second term, defeating [[Democratic Party (United States)|Democratic]] Congressman [[Beto O'Rourke]]. The primary was held on March 6, 2018, the first primary of the 2018 season.<ref>{{cite web|title=Texas 2018 General Election|url=https://www.thegreenpapers.com/G18/TX|website=The Green Papers|access-date=December 10, 2017}}</ref>

No Democrat has won a general election for statewide office in Texas since [[Bob Bullock]] was reelected as lieutenant governor in 1994, with election forecasters declaring it a safe Republican seat at the beginning of the 2018 cycle.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.texasobserver.org/meet-the-merry-band-of-texas-democrats-running-for-statewide-office/|title=Meet the Merry Band of Texas Democrats Running for Statewide Office|last=Miller|first=Justin|date=June 6, 2018|publisher=Texas Observer|access-date=September 28, 2018}}</ref> However, O'Rourke gradually closed the gap.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/how-much-trouble-is-ted-cruz-really-in/|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180831152924/https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/how-much-trouble-is-ted-cruz-really-in/|url-status=dead|archive-date=August 31, 2018|title=How Much Trouble Is Ted Cruz Really In?|last=Mehta|first=Dhrumil|date=August 31, 2018|publisher=538|access-date=September 28, 2018}}</ref> Leading up to the election, the race was considered unexpectedly competitive.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.vox.com/2018/9/12/17841716/ted-cruz-beto-orourke-senate-midterm|title=Ted Cruz's surprisingly competitive battle against Beto O'Rourke, explained|last=Golshan|first=Tara|date=September 12, 2018|publisher=Vox|access-date=September 28, 2018}}</ref>

On Election Day, Cruz defeated O'Rourke<ref>{{Cite news |url=https://www.cbsnews.com/live-news/texas-senate-race-beto-orourke-ted-cruz-election-results-polls-vote-count-2018-11-06-live-updates/ |title=Ted Cruz wins Texas Senate race, fending off challenge from Beto O'Rourke |date=2018-11-06 |work=CBS News |access-date=2018-11-07 |language=en}}</ref> by a margin of just under 215,000 votes, or 2.6 percent; this was the closest U.S. Senate race in Texas since [[1978 United States Senate election in Texas|1978]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.houstonchronicle.com/news/politics/texas/article/Ted-Cruz-s-margin-of-victory-over-Beto-13448497.php|title=Ted Cruz's margin of victory over Beto O'Rourke was even slimmer than we thought - HoustonChronicle.com|date=December 6, 2018|website=www.houstonchronicle.com}}</ref>

==Background==
{{Main|Politics of Texas}}
In 2012, after a stunning upset victory in the Republican primary, then-Solicitor General of Texas [[Ted Cruz]] defeated former member of the Texas House of Representatives [[Paul Sadler]] by a 16-point margin (56%–40%). Texas has not elected a Democratic senator since 1988. As conservatives began turning to the [[Republican Party (United States)|Republican Party]] in once strongly Democratic areas, Democratic voters in the state were largely based in the [[Hispanic and Latino Americans|majority-Hispanic]] communities in [[South Texas|Southern Texas]] and in populous metropolitan cities, such as [[Houston]], [[Austin, Texas|Austin]], [[San Antonio]], and [[Dallas]], as well as the heavily Hispanic city of [[El Paso]] on the state's western tip, which O'Rourke represented in the U.S. House.

Since 1990, Texas has voted for Republican statewide candidates in nearly all elections, whether it be presidential, gubernatorial, or senatorial, often by large margins. In 1998, Governor [[George W. Bush]] won re-election by 37 points over his Democratic challenger, [[Garry Mauro]]. In 2000, Governor Bush won Texas by 21 points over Vice President [[Al Gore]]. In 2004, President Bush won Texas over Senator [[John Kerry]] by 23 points, winning rural areas by landslide margins, capturing urban zones, and coming very close to winning the [[Hispanics and Latinos in Texas|Latino]] vote (49% to Kerry's 50%). Democrat [[Barack Obama]] lost by 7.27 percentage points in 2008 against [[John McCain]], and 15.8 points in 2012, against [[Mitt Romney]], respectively. In 2016, [[Donald Trump]] defeated Democrat [[Hillary Clinton]] by a 9-point margin, demonstrating a possible shift away from the Safe Republican status it had held for over a decade. This has led Democrats to begin targeting Texas as a potential future swing state. It should also be noted that Ted Cruz defeated Donald Trump in the Texas Republican primary for U.S. president in 2016.

As of June 2018, Senator Cruz held a 49%–44% approval rating among Texans in a state Donald Trump won by 9 points against Hillary Clinton in 2016. Among groups that tend to affiliate themselves more with the [[Democratic Party (United States)|Democratic Party]], Senator Cruz held a 29% approval rating among Hispanics, 37% among women, and 42% among college-educated voters.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://morningconsult.com/2018/04/12/americas-most-and-least-popular-senators/|title=America's Most and Least Popular Senators|work=[[Morning Consult]]|last=Easley|first=Cameron|date=April 12, 2018|access-date=June 30, 2018}}</ref>

===Results===
On November 6, 2018, Ted Cruz defeated Beto O'Rourke. However, O'Rourke performed unexpectedly well, outperforming pre-election polling.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://abcnews.go.com/Politics/ted-cruz-thwarts-challenge-democratic-insurgent-beto-orourke/story?id=58854838|title=Ted Cruz thwarts challenge from Democratic insurgent Beto O'Rourke in tight Senate race, ABC News projects|work=ABC News|date=November 7, 2018|access-date=November 7, 2018}}</ref> In addition, O'Rourke flipped numerous counties that Donald Trump carried in 2016, including [[Williamson County, Texas|Williamson]] (includes [[Round Rock, Texas|Round Rock]] and [[Georgetown, Texas|Georgetown]]), historically conservative [[Tarrant County, Texas|Tarrant]] (includes [[Fort Worth, Texas|Fort Worth]] and suburbs within the [[DFW metroplex]]), [[Jefferson County, Texas|Jefferson]] (includes [[Beaumont, Texas|Beaumont]] and [[Port Arthur, Texas|Port Arthur]]), [[Nueces County, Texas|Nueces]] (includes [[Corpus Christi, Texas|Corpus Christi]]), sparsely populated [[Brewster County, Texas|Brewster]] (includes [[Big Bend National Park]]), and [[Hays County, Texas|Hays]] (includes [[San Marcos, Texas|San Marcos]]). Cruz won only one county that voted for Hillary Clinton in 2016, sparsely populated [[Kenedy County, Texas|Kenedy]] (coastal region south of [[Corpus Christi, Texas|Corpus Christi]]).
