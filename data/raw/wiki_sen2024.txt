The '''2024 United States Senate election in Texas''' was held on November 5, 2024, to elect a member of the [[United States Senate]] to represent the state of [[Texas]]. [[Republican Party (United States)|Republican]] Senator [[Ted Cruz]] was re-elected to a third term, defeating [[Democratic Party (United States)|Democratic]] U.S. Representative [[Colin Allred]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Yoon |first=Robert |date=2024-11-06 |title=Why AP called the Texas Senate race for Ted Cruz |url=https://apnews.com/article/texas-senate-race-call-explainer-cruz-allred-2d942196ee0dee5d4c77fa3b88d20196 |access-date=2024-11-06 |website=AP News |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Weaver |first=Al |date=2024-11-06 |title=Ted Cruz wins Texas Senate reelection race, defeating Colin Allred |url=https://thehill.com/homenews/campaign/4964222-ted-cruz-wins-texas-senate-race/ |access-date=2024-11-06 |website=The Hill |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Garcia |first=Eric |date=2024-11-06 |title=Ted Cruz evades Democratic efforts to unseat him again in Texas |url=https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/americas/us-politics/texas-senate-results-ted-cruz-colin-allred-b2637827.html |access-date=2024-11-06 |website=The Independent |language=en}}</ref> The primary election took place on March 5, 2024, during [[Super Tuesday]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=2024 State Primary Election Dates |url=https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/2024-state-primary-election-dates |website=[[National Conference of State Legislatures]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231110155538/https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/2024-state-primary-election-dates |archive-date=November 10, 2023}}</ref>

Early polling showed Cruz as a clear favorite, but polls closer to the election showed a closer race. Cruz ultimately outperformed polling and expectations, and won re-election by 8.49%, improving on his 2018 margin by six points and flipping thirteen counties.<ref name="auto">{{cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2024/09/24/us/politics/ted-cruz-colin-allred-texas-senate.html |title=Ted Cruz Is Again Fighting for Political Survival in Texas |access-date=September 25, 2024 |work=The New York Times |date=September 24, 2024}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Reporter |first=Martha McHardy US News |date=2024-09-22 |title=Could Ted Cruz actually lose in Texas? |url=https://www.newsweek.com/texas-senate-election-ted-cruz-colin-allred-1957284 |access-date=2024-09-23 |website=Newsweek |language=en}}</ref> 

== Background ==
Texas is generally considered to be a [[Red states and blue states|Republican stronghold]], having not elected a [[Texas Democratic Party|Democrat]] to any statewide office since 1994.<ref name="texastribune.org">{{Cite web |title=Republican victories show Texas is still far from turning blue |url=https://www.texastribune.org/2022/11/09/texas-election-results/ |website=The Texas Tribune |date=November 9, 2022 |language=en|quote=As large as the cities are and how Democratic that they are, Texas Democrats still don't have a way to get past that red wall of rural West Texas, [Drew Landry] said. Rural Texas still rules the day. I was seeing some very, very close numbers before a lot of the rural counties reported [election returns], and once they did, it just blew the door open for Abbott.}}</ref>
[[Republican Party of Texas|Republicans]] control both U.S. Senate seats, all statewide offices, both houses of the [[Texas Legislature]], and a large majority in Texas's [[United States House of Representatives|U.S. House]] [[Texas's congressional delegations|congressional delegation]].
Cruz was first elected in [[2012 United States Senate election in Texas|2012]], defeating [[Paul Sadler]] by 16 points and was reelected in [[2018 United States Senate election in Texas|2018]] by less than 3 points, narrowly defeating [[Beto O'Rourke]].<ref>{{Cite news |title=Texas |language=en-US |work=The New York Times |url=https://www.nytimes.com/elections/2012/results/states/texas.html |access-date=2023-04-16 |issn=0362-4331}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Texas Senate Election Results 2018 |url=https://www.politico.com/election-results/2018/texas/senate/ |access-date=2023-04-16 |website=[[POLITICO]] |date=November 7, 2018 |language=en}}</ref> The close elections in [[2018 Texas elections|2018]] prompted many [[Psephology|electoral analysts]] to speculate that Texas could become a [[swing state]], but in the [[2020 Texas elections|2020]] and [[2022 Texas elections|2022]] elections, Republicans increased their margins of victory.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Goodman |first=J. David |date=2022-11-09 |title=Texas Democrats Look to a Future Beyond Beto |language=en-US |work=The New York Times |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2022/11/09/us/texas-beto-orourke-abbott.html |access-date=2023-04-16 |issn=0362-4331}}</ref><ref name="texastribune.org"/>
This race was considered to generally favor Cruz, but some considered the race to have the potential to become competitive.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Choi |first=Matthew |date=2023-03-30 |title=Why Ted Cruz's next reelection path looks smoother with Beto O'Rourke behind him |url=https://www.texastribune.org/2023/03/30/ted-cruz-colin-allred-beto-orourke-senate-2024/ |access-date=2023-04-16 |website=The Texas Tribune |language=en}}</ref>

Cruz won a majority of [[Hispanic and Latino Americans|Hispanic and Latino]] voters, particularly those living on the [[Mexico–United States border|border with Mexico]] who had traditionally supported Democratic candidates; the ''[[NBC News]]'' exit poll showed 52% of Latinos supported Cruz, a 17-point increase from 2018.<ref>{{cite news |title=Hispanic voters shift to the right, leaving Texas Democrats seeking answers |first=Joseph |last=Morton |work=[[The Dallas Morning News]] |date=November 7, 2024 |access-date=November 22, 2024 |url=https://www.dallasnews.com/news/politics/2024/11/07/hispanic-voters-shift-to-the-right-leaving-texas-democrats-seeking-answers/}}</ref>

Allred overperformed [[Kamala Harris]] in the [[2024 United States presidential election in Texas|concurrent presidential election in Texas]] by 5.5 points, receiving nearly 200,000 votes more than Harris and performing better than she did in the largely Hispanic [[Rio Grande Valley]]. He carried the Rio Grande Valley counties of [[Cameron County, Texas|Cameron]], [[Duval County, Texas|Duval]], [[Hidalgo County, Texas|Hidalgo]], [[Webb County, Texas|Webb]] ([[Laredo, Texas|Laredo]]), and [[Willacy County, Texas|Willacy]], as well as [[Williamson County, Texas|Williamson]] and [[Tarrant County, Texas|Tarrant]] counties, which Trump simultaneously carried.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Guo |first=By Kayla |date=2025-07-01 |title=Texas Democrat Colin Allred launches 2026 U.S. Senate campaign |url=https://www.texastribune.org/2025/07/01/colin-allred-texas-us-senate-democrat-2026-cornyn-paxton/ |access-date=2025-07-01 |website=[[The Texas Tribune]] |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Epstein |first=Reid J. |date=2025-07-01 |title=Colin Allred Will Run Again for Senate in Texas |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2025/07/01/us/politics/colin-allred-texas-senate.html |access-date=2025-07-01 |work=[[The New York Times]] |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref>
